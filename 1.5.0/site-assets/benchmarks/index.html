Reading the charts

Change is (after − before) ÷ before × 100. Each pair measures a commit and its parent in the same benchmark job. This controls for hardware differences between runners. The shaded band indicates estimated typical variation. Small real changes can fall inside it, and measurement variation can exceed it. These are separate before/after comparisons and are never added together.

The estimated bands are ±7% for build time and ±5% for startup. They were chosen from 423 pairs per metric recorded through September 8, 2026. The robust spread of paired changes is about 3.5% for build and 2.0% for startup. The build band is about twice that spread; the startup band allows a little more margin and contains 94% of its historical changes. These histories include real code changes, so the bands are guides for inspection. They do not establish statistical significance. The estimates stay fixed when you change the history range.

The trend line is the median of the current and previous 9 after measurements. It starts once 10 pairs are available. It uses the full history before the selected range is applied. Changes in runner conditions can affect this trend too.

The summary uses only the selected history. Measured time change compares the median of the first 10 current-commit timings with the median of the last 10. Histories with fewer than 20 pairs use smaller, separate windows. At least two pairs are needed to compare endpoints. Median same-run change is the median of the individual paired percentage changes across the selected history.

A diamond flags a possible change in CI runner speed. Both values must be at least 20% above or below their respective medians from the previous 10 pairs. The same-run change must also fall within the estimated variation band for that benchmark. At least 5 earlier pairs are required. This context always uses the full history, including pairs outside the selected range.

The published history contains no runner hardware or capacity details. Shared movement can come from a slower or faster runner, workload changes, tool updates, or measurement noise. A diamond is a clue to inspect; it does not confirm a hardware change.
